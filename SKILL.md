# The Partner 100 Starter

Partnerology's version of the Dream 100, cut down to one job: find the first 25 people who already have your customers, and know exactly how to open with each one. Works in Claude, ChatGPT, Hermes or any AI that can research the web. Paste it in and say "start my Partner 100".

## Where it comes from
Chet Holmes called it the Dream 100 and built it for sales: pick the best fit names, pursue only those, value first, patiently. Russell Brunson moved it from buyers to audiences: your Dream 100 is the people who have already gathered your customers. List owners, podcast hosts, community leaders, top promoters. You can buy your way into their audience with ads, or work your way in with a relationship. Ads cost whether they work or not. A partner costs nothing until they bring a sale.

Say it this way: you do not need more people. You need the people who already have the people.

## Who it suits
Owners with an offer that already sells. If the offer is not converting yet, say so kindly. Partners cannot fix an offer that does not sell.

## The conversation first
Before any research, read the owner's website. Then ask these, one at a time, and keep the answers:
1. Who is your customer, in one sentence, and what are they trying to get done right now?
2. Who do you already wish were promoting you? Name them.
3. Who do you know? Past collaborators, hosts of shows you have been on, customers with an audience, people in your masterminds and communities. Ask "who comes to mind?" Never ask for a contact file or an address book.
4. What do you want your name associated with, and who would you never want recommending you, however big their audience?
5. What can you give before you ask? A guest spot, an introduction, a piece of work, a result worth sharing.

## Agree three categories
A category is a relationship to the customer, not a platform. Agree three with the owner before researching a single name. The ones that turn up for most brands: people who teach the customer, people who already promote offers like this one, service firms whose clients need what the brand sells, and people with a show, newsletter or community the customer follows. Use the owner's words.

## Find 50, keep 25
Research twice what you need so you can be picky. Sources, in order:
1. The owner's dream list and warm network. Warm beats big.
2. People who already talk about the brand. Search its name.
3. People who recommend competing offers.
4. Shoulder audiences: people who serve the same customer with something different.
5. Podcasts the customer listens to. The host is a candidate. So is every repeat guest.

Spread the 25 across the three categories. Twenty five names in one category is one bet.

## Sort them
- **Tier A.** The audience is the brand's customer almost exactly, the reach is real, and they have shown they do promotional deals. Five to eight names. The owner approaches these personally.
- **Tier B.** Strong fit, smaller reach or cooler relationship. The rest.
- **Warmth.** Hot: they already promote offers like this. Warm: the owner knows them or is one introduction away. Cold: no relationship yet. Write the reason beside the label. "Warm" with no reason is a guess.
- **Values screen.** Look at how each person behaves in public: what they promote, how they sell, how they speak about others. Pass, review or fail, with what you saw and where. Judge conduct you can point to. Never screen on who someone is: religion, politics, background or any personal characteristic.

## What every record holds
Name and link. Where they publish and roughly how many follow. Category. Tier. Warmth with reason. Values result with evidence. Fit reason, one or two specific sentences. Fit considerations, the honest other side. The way in: a mutual contact by name, their partner page, a shared community. The first touch angle: something you noticed in their recent work, the bridge to their audience, and no ask. A source link for every fact.

A record with an empty field is not finished and does not count toward the 25.

## The first ten
From the 25, name ten to work first: Hot and Warm from Tier A, then the strongest of B. One line each on why they go first. Then ask the owner to approve every name. Any name they are not glad to see, replace. Nothing gets written to anyone until every name has their yes.

## Rules
- Never open with the ask. A first message leads with a specific compliment tied to something they published recently, a piece of work made for them, or an introduction they would want.
- Cold names get two to four weeks of the owner showing up in public before any direct message.
- The owner shows up as themselves. You find the moment and prepare the words. You never post or message as them.
- One first impression. No template, no bulk send. If you cannot write something specific and true to one person, they are not ready to be contacted.
- Honest fit notes on every name.
- No promises about results.

## Deliver it as
A table the owner can open and share: Name, Link, Category, Tier, Warmth and reason, Values, Fit reason, Fit considerations, Way in, First touch angle, Sources. Then the first ten with the one line each. Then one page: the three categories, the five values, and the one place where many of these names gather, if you found one. One room warms a dozen names faster than a year of messages.

## Pitfalls
- Filling the list with the biggest names. Reach is not fit, and the biggest are the least likely to answer.
- A values screen that is a formality. One wrong partner costs more than ten missed ones.
- An angle that would fit anyone. Rewrite it or drop the name.
- Mixing this with a list of dream customers. Keep them apart.
- Stopping at the list. Finding them is the start. Showing up every day is the strategy.
